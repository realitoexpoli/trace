Animating

There is no timeline. You decide what the slide looks like at each click, and Tracé animates between those moments.

On this page

Two ways to animate

  • Clicks: objects appear, change and leave on click 1, 2, 3… within one slide.
  • Slides: duplicate a slide and change the copy. Everything that moved, grew, turned or changed animates when you go to the next slide.

You can mix both freely. Clicks are best for building up one picture; duplicated slides are best for a sequence of states, like a story board.

Building a slide click by click

Select an object and look at Animation in the settings. Appears says when it comes in: With the slide, or On click 1, On click 2… The last option, marked (new), adds a click to the slide.

Leaves, When it moves, Keeps moving and Emphasis are folded under Leaving, moving and looping: click it to open it. To practise, open Help → Tour: change things on a click or Tour: animate between slides.

Objects that appear on the same click come in together. To bring them in one after another, tick Objects that appear together come in one after another in the slide settings.

Changing an object on a click

This is the heart of Tracé. Click a step in the clicks bar under the slide, for example Click 1. The slide now shows that moment, and whatever you change animates on that click: drag an object, resize or turn it, recolour it, or change any value in its settings.

  • Values animate too: the number of Riemann rectangles, a slider value, Curve drawn so far, the number of series terms, a wave’s phase, a matrix, a 3D view. Set a different value on each click and it changes smoothly.
  • Follow a path: while a click is selected, Travels can send the object along a curve, circle or other shape on the slide instead of in a straight line. A dashed line shows the route.
  • Undo this click’s changes puts the object back to how it was before that click.
  • Click the selected step again to go back to editing the slide as a whole.

The clicks bar

Each step shows what happens on it: +2 means two objects appear, −1 one leaves, ~1 one changes, and a magnifier means the view zooms. When a step is selected you also get:

  • Lasts: how many seconds this click’s animation takes.
  • by itself: the step plays on its own after the previous one, without a click. Use it for a sequence that runs by itself.
  • View: zoom into part of the slide on this click. Frame the selected object zooms to it; Full slide zooms back out.
  • ▶ Play: plays just this step.

+ Click adds a step at the end. If an object is selected, it will appear on the new click.

Animating between slides

  1. Finish a slide, then press Duplicate under the slide list.
  2. On the copy, change what should change: move the point, edit the equation, turn the 3D view, add a new object, delete one.
  3. Press Preview, or present: going to the copy animates every change. Objects that only exist on the new slide enter; objects that are gone leave.

The line under each slide in the list sums up what will animate, for example “2 move, 1 morph, 1 new”.

Speed and feel

Click an empty part of the slide to see the slide settings:

  • Transition length: how long the move into this slide takes.
  • Motion feel: Smooth, Steady (constant speed), Snappy (fast start), Bouncy (slight overshoot) and more.
  • Background (whole deck) and Show grid, a grid that helps you line things up and never shows when presenting.

Tips

  • Use Preview often. It plays exactly what the audience will see when this slide starts.
  • Keep one idea per click. If a click changes five things, the audience will not know where to look.
  • To draw a graph after its axes are on screen, show the graph with the slide, set Curve drawn so far to 0, then set it to 1 on click 1.
  • Animations that keep moving (Keeps moving) keep running while the next steps play, so a spinning object never stops abruptly.
