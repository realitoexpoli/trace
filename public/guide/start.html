Opening the editor

From the home page, press Open the editor. Tracé runs in your web browser on a computer, tablet or phone; Chrome or Edge work best. You do not need an account to start.

Learn by doing: the interactive tutorial

The quickest way to learn Tracé is the tutorial built into the editor. Press Tour at the top (it is always there). You see every chapter with a tick for the ones you have done, and a button to continue where you left off.

Each chapter points at the place to click and waits for you to do it, so you learn with your own hands. If you are stuck, Do it for me does the step for you. Chapters run in a Tutorial practice project, so your own work is never touched, and it stays on this device (it is not saved to your account).

The screen

  1. Project name. Click it to rename the project. The arrow next to it opens the project menu: new, duplicate, import, save as a file, delete.
  2. Add menus. Write (text, equations, lists, code, tables, counters), Draw (shapes, arrows, images), Geometry, Plot (graphs, curves, vectors), 3D and Science.
  3. Slides. Click a slide to edit it. The line under each one says what animates into it from the slide before, for example “1 morph, 3 new, 5 out”.
  4. The slide. Click an object to select it, drag it to move it. Corner handles resize, side handles stretch, the round handle on top rotates. See Selecting objects.
  5. Clicks bar. The steps of this slide: Opens is what you see when the slide starts, then Click 1, Click 2… Press + Click to add one.
  6. Settings. Everything about the selected object, in order: what it shows and how it looks, then Animation, with a one-line summary such as “On click 1, drawn”. Less common settings are folded away under Position, size and turn and Leaving, moving and looping; click a heading to open it, and Tracé remembers which ones you keep open. With nothing selected, it shows the slide’s settings and a list of the objects on the slide.
  7. Share. Get a link anyone can watch (needs an account).
  8. Present. Plays the deck full screen from the current slide.
  9. Account. Sign in, or your account menu once signed in.

Tour opens the interactive tutorial; Help next to it opens this guide. Undo (or Ctrl+Z) takes back the last change. Preview plays the animation into the current slide without leaving the editor.

Selecting objects

Click anywhere on an object to select it. You do not have to hit a thin line exactly: Tracé picks the object nearest to your pointer.

  • See before you click. When the pointer is over an object, a dashed outline and its name show what a click will select.
  • Objects on top of each other. Click once to select the top one. Hold Alt and click again to select the one under it, and so on. Once an object is selected, dragging anywhere inside it moves it, even if another object is in front.
  • Go through them with the keyboard. With an object selected, Tab selects the next object and Shift+Tab the previous one.
  • Pick from a list. Click an empty part of the slide: the settings list every object On this slide. Point at a name to see where it is, click it to select it.
  • Bring to front, send to back. At the bottom of the settings, Bring to front and Send to back change which object is on top.

Selecting is also how you edit: double-click a text or an equation on the slide to jump straight to its box in the settings.

Your first slide, step by step

You will make a slide that opens with an equation, draws its graph on the first click, and lets you change the graph while presenting. Tour → Your first animated slide guides you through the same steps on screen.

  1. Start a blank project. Open the project menu (the arrow next to the project name) and choose New project.
  2. Add a title. Write → Text. Type your title in the Text box on the right. Double-clicking a text on the slide also takes you to that box.
  3. Add an equation. Write → Equation. In the Equation box, replace the example with y = sin(k x). Type it like on a calculator; Tracé writes it properly on the slide.
  4. Plot it. Press Plot this equation as a graph under the equation box. A graph appears, linked to the equation: change the equation later and the graph follows.
  5. Make k adjustable. Select the equation again. In Plotting, Tracé says it reads the equation as y against x, holding k fixed. Tick Live slider next to k and choose its range.
  6. Draw the graph on a click. Select the graph. Under Animation, set Appears to On click 1 and Enters with to Draw.
  7. Present. Press Present. The slide opens with the title and the equation; click once and the graph draws itself. Drag the k slider at the bottom to change the wave in front of your audience. Press Esc to come back.

Example decks

The Examples menu holds ready-made decks you can play and take apart:

  • Tour: everything Tracé can do: 14 slides showing almost every feature.
  • Lecture: how a signal travels: an 11-slide physics lecture.
  • Cours : le brouillage des communications: a 15-slide course in French.
  • Radar jamming in 3D: adds three slides with a 3D surface to the project you have open.

The first three open as new projects, so they never overwrite your work.

Where your work is kept

Every change is saved straight away in this browser, on this device. To open your decks on other devices, share them or keep them safe if the browser’s data is cleared, sign in: see Saving and sharing.
