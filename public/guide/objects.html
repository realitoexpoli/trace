Objects and equations

Every object on a slide has settings on the right. Most of them can also change on a click, which is how you animate them.

Adding objects

Open a menu at the top and pick an object. It appears in the middle of the slide, selected, with its settings on the right.

MenuWhat is in it
WriteText, Equation, Bullet list, Code, Matrix, Table, Counter
DrawImage, Circle, Ellipse, Rectangle, Polygon, Star, Arc, Sector or ring, Line, Arrow, Dot, Brace, Cross mark
GeometryLabelled point, Triangle, Angle, Number plane, Venn diagram
PlotGraph, Parametric curve, Polar plot, Number line, Vector, Vector field, Complex plane
3DSphere, Cube, Cuboid, Ellipsoid, Cylinder, Cone, Prism, Pyramid, Torus, the regular polyhedra, 3D arrow, 3D axes, a 3D scene with several shapes
ScienceWave, Phasor, Signal and spectrum, Chart, Network, plus four signal-jamming simulations (symbols in noise, J/S map, frequency hopping, spread spectrum)

Moving and resizing

  • Select: click the object. Alt+click selects the one underneath, Tab goes to the next one; see Selecting objects.
  • Move: drag the object, or nudge it with the arrow keys (Shift for bigger steps).
  • Resize: drag a corner handle. Side handles stretch shapes and graphs in one direction.
  • Rotate: drag the round handle above the object.
  • Exact values: open Position, size and turn in the settings and type position x and y, scale, rotation, opacity.
  • In front or behind: Bring to front and Send to back at the bottom of the settings.
  • Duplicate: Ctrl+D, or Duplicate at the bottom of the settings. Delete: Delete.

If the same object is on several slides (because you duplicated the slide), the settings say On 2 slides: changes between them animate. Changing it on one slide does not change the others.

Text

Choose the font, size, bold, italic, underline, alignment and line spacing in the bar at the top of the settings, as in PowerPoint. To format only some words, select them in the text box and press a button or a colour.

Bullet lists and tables work the same way. A list can reveal its items one by one: animate Items shown on successive clicks. Tables have Rows shown and code has Lines shown.

Equations

An equation can be written in two ways, set by Write it as:

  • Normal maths: type it like on a calculator, for example y = A sin(k x) e^(-x/4). Tracé typesets it and can plot it.
  • LaTeX: for anything else, such as integrals, matrices or chemistry notation.

Two settings help you explain an equation step by step: Show only the first terms reveals a long sum term by term (animate it on clicks), and Highlight a term colours one part, for example P_j.

When a formula has a mistake, a red note under the box says what is wrong and the slide keeps the last correct version.

From equation to graph

Press Plot this equation as a graph. The button adapts to what you typed: a pair like (cos(t), sin(2t)) becomes a curve, F = (-y, x) a vector field, v = (2, 1) a vector, and an equation in x and y a 3D surface.

The graph stays linked to the equation: edit the equation and the graph updates on every slide. Under Plotting, Tracé tells you how it reads the equation (“y against x, holding A, k fixed”). Each fixed letter has a value and a Live slider box: tick it to get a slider under the slide and while presenting. Several equations that use the same letter share one slider.

Graphs

Select a graph to see its options. Fit ranges to the curve picks sensible axes. Other options include:

  • A point on the curve: Mark a point on the curve, then add a Tangent line at the point, a Secant line (animate Δx down to 0 to show a derivative), or Trace the curve up to the point. Slider for the point while presenting lets you move it live.
  • Area and integrals: Shade the area under the curve between two x values, and Riemann rectangles: animate their number from 4 to 40 to show the integral appear.
  • Series: set Curve to Fourier or Taylor series and animate Number of terms.
  • Drawing the curve: Curve drawn so far from 0 to 1 on a click draws the curve while the axes stay put.
  • A logarithmic vertical axis, axis names, a curve label, and an equation in x and y such as a circle.

Drawings and the library

For anything that is not in the menus (a rocket, a magnet, a flask), open Library at the top. It holds ready-made drawings for maths and science slides, grouped into Space, Motion, Physics, Chemistry, Nature and Everyday. Type in the search box (for example rocket, magnet or newton) and press Enter to add the first match.

A drawing behaves like any other object: it can draw itself when it appears, move, grow and turn on a click, follow a path, or morph into another object.

  • Colours: Its own colours keeps the drawing as it is; One colour paints it in a single colour you choose, which can also change on a click.
  • Swap it: Change drawing… replaces the picture and keeps its position, size and animation.

Your own drawings

Any drawing saved as an SVG file works: from Inkscape, Illustrator, Figma, Canva or an icon website. In Library, choose Import an SVG file…, or drag the file onto the slide, or copy SVG code and paste it (Ctrl+V). Tracé keeps the shapes and colours; text inside the file and photos are left out (turn text into outlines in your drawing app first). Imported drawings are saved with the project, so they are there when you share it.

Only use drawings you made or are allowed to use. Many icon sites say which licence each icon has.

3D

The 3D menu adds solids (sphere, cube, cone, torus, polyhedra…) or a set of 3D axes. On the free plan you get the basic shapes: sphere, cube, cuboid, ellipsoid, cylinder, cone, prism and pyramid. Items marked PRO (torus, the regular polyhedra, 3D arrows, 3D axes and surfaces, 3D scenes, and views that keep turning) need Pro.

3D axes can show a surface from an equation, a parametric surface, an implicit surface such as x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 4, or a 3D curve.

  • Turn the view: hold Shift and drag the 3D object on the slide, or use Turn and Tilt. While presenting, just drag.
  • Animate the camera: change Turn or Tilt on a click, or set Keeps moving to Keep turning the view (PRO).
  • Tick Turn and tilt sliders while presenting to give yourself sliders on stage.

Counters

Write → Counter shows a number that counts up or down. Its Value comes from setting can follow an equation’s result or the height of a marked point on a graph, so the number changes as you move a slider. Tick Count automatically when the slide starts to make it count from one value to another, and add text before or after it (for example “Speed:” and “km/h”).

Other objects

  • Geometry: move a triangle’s corner and its labels and angle marks follow; an angle can show a square mark at 90°; Venn diagrams shade unions, intersections and differences.
  • Number plane: apply a matrix and watch the grid transform. Complex plane: animate Apply the map from 0 to 1 for maps like z^2.
  • Science: waves (travelling, standing, packets), phasors that trace a sine, spectra, charts, networks (circle, tree or neural-network layouts) and the signal simulations.
  • Images: Draw → Image, then choose a picture from your computer.
  • Code with syntax colours for Python, JavaScript, MATLAB and C, with line-by-line reveal and highlight.
